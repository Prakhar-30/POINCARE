The problem

Liquidity providers lose money precisely when markets trend

There is an invisible tax on every pool, and it has a name: Loss-Versus-Rebalancing. Whenever the price moves, arbitrageurs trade against the pool's stale quote and keep the difference. The Milionis–Moallemi–Roughgarden–Zhang result tells us exactly what drives it:

LVR rate ≈ ½ · σ² · marginal liquidity

Notice what is missing from that equation: the fee. Every fee-tweaking hook is pulling a lever that simply is not in the formula. And notice what drives it: sustained, one-directional moves. Chop barely hurts LPs. Trends drain them.

A normal pool during a trend

The curve is frozen, so it offers the same terms to the arbitrageur draining it as to the trader helping it. LPs pay the full tax, block after block, until the trend ends.

What would actually help

A pool that can tell a real trend from noise, and defends itself only while one is happening.

That judgement call is the hard part, and it is the whole project
Poincaré02
The insight

"Has a real trend started?" was solved in 1954. Nobody brought it on-chain.

Quickest Change Detection

Statisticians have spent seventy years on exactly this question: how do you detect a regime change as fast as possible without crying wolf? Their workhorse answer is the CUSUM statistic. It quietly accumulates evidence every step and fires the moment that evidence crosses a threshold.

St = max(0, St−1 + ℓt − k)  →  alarm when St ≥ h

And it comes with a proof: under Lorden's criterion, CUSUM minimises the worst-case detection delay for any tolerated false-alarm rate. This is not a heuristic that felt right. It is the best possible answer to the speed-versus-false-alarms question.

Why an AMM should care

The firing moment depends on the data, not the clock. A strong trend crosses the threshold in a few blocks, while noise never gets there at all. So there is no fixed countdown for an attacker to study and game.

Nobody else does this

We scanned the entire 562-hook UHI directory and found zero uses of CUSUM or any change-point method. Everything out there triggers on moving averages and fixed thresholds, which is exactly the kind of signal an attacker can predict.

To our knowledge, Poincaré is the first AMM whose behaviour is governed by a provably optimal sequential change detector.

Poincaré03
The design

A brain that decides, a curve that acts, a fee that breathes

The brain

Two-sided CUSUM detector

It runs on the pool's own log-returns, sampled once per block from pre-swap reserves so flash tricks never reach it. A directional-efficiency gate confirms the move is a march rather than a thrash, and clipped increments keep fat-tailed crypto returns from fooling it.

The actuator

A directional spread that LPs keep

When a trend is confirmed, flow pushing with the trend pays a small, capped spread, and that value goes straight to the LPs it would otherwise have been taken from. Flow that stabilises the pool trades at the full base price, because punishing your friends is bad business.

The revenue

A fee generated from volatility

In calm markets LPs still earn: the base fee is computed each block from the pool's own realized volatility, so it tightens when things are quiet and widens when they are not. Never a constant someone picked in a meeting.

Once per block: pool price → log-return → CUSUM + gate → bounded, rate-limited κ → spread on the toxic side only

The signal is the pool's own price and nothing else. No oracle to corrupt, no keeper to bribe, no AVS to trust, and every parameter is injected and calibrated rather than hard-coded.

Poincaré04
How it behaves

It ignores the noise, then commits when the evidence is real

Calm

Evidence hovers near zero, so the curve stays symmetric and deep. Everyone gets the best price the pool can offer.

A trend begins

The statistic starts climbing, but the detector holds its fire because early moves look exactly like noise. Crying wolf here would tax honest traders for nothing.

Detection

Evidence crosses the threshold at a moment set by the data, and only then does the curve lean, capped and rate-limited so it can never snap.

The trend fades

Returns shrink, the gate closes, and the lean ramps back down with hysteresis so it never flaps.

Poincaré05
Why it can't be gamed

Faking a trend means genuinely moving the market, at your own expense

1 · No countdown to study

The firing time depends on the data, and the detector samples once per block from settled reserves, so a flash loan that unwinds inside a block is simply invisible to it.

2 · Nothing to win

The spread only ever makes trades worse, never better. The "soft" side trades at the plain base price, so an attacker who fakes a trend finds zero edge waiting on the other side.

3 · Arbitrageurs collect the toll

Pushing the price off fair value opens a gap that arbitrageurs harvest the moment the attacker stops. The chart shows their cost climbing the entire time, and it buys them nothing.

All three layers are simulated end-to-end against the real PoolManager in our test suite: fake-trend round trips lose money, flash spikes never reach the detector.

That is the frontier that matters: protection per unit of collateral damage.

Poincaré11
Scope, honestly

What it does, and what we refuse to pretend

Where it shines

Liquid, free-floating, volatile pairs like ETH/USDC, because that is where directional LVR actually lives, the price series is clean, and moving the market costs real money. Pegged pairs degrade gracefully: the curve just stays symmetric, and a depeg is simply a trend it leans into.

It removes the avoidable arbitrage tax, not market risk itself. LPs still hold the assets, and we say so on the landing page rather than in a footnote.

We found our own exploit, and shipped around it

The obvious version of this idea, bending the curve's depth per direction, turns out to be drainable. We proved it with a concrete counterexample: a bot buys the deep branch, sells back on the shallow one, and walks off with pool value. So we shipped the spread construction, which is safe because it can only ever make a round trip worse, and we keep the depth lever on the roadmap behind the safety analysis it deserves.

Same discipline on the adaptive detector: it is built, simulated against volatility attacks, and it backtests at twice the LVR cut, but it will not guard real value until its worst-case bound is derived.
