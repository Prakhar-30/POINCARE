The problem

LPs don't bleed on volatility. They bleed on direction.

There is an invisible tax on every pool, and it has a name: Loss-Versus-Rebalancing. Whenever the price moves, arbitrageurs trade against the pool's stale quote and keep the difference. The Milionis–Moallemi–Roughgarden–Zhang identity pins down what drives it:

LVR rate ≈ ½ · σ² · marginal liquidity

Notice what is missing: the fee. Every fee-tweaking hook pulls a lever that is not in the formula. And notice what drives it — sustained, one-directional moves. Chop barely hurts LPs. Trends drain them.

Both paths take the same set of step sizes — only the order of the signs differs. The one that goes nowhere barely leaks value; the one that trends is arbitraged the whole way.

Poincaré02
The insight · the engine

"Has a real trend started?" was solved in 1954. Nobody brought it on-chain.

Quickest Change Detection

Statisticians spent seventy years on exactly this question: detect a regime change as fast as possible without crying wolf. The workhorse answer is the CUSUM statistic — it accumulates evidence each block and fires the moment that evidence crosses a threshold.

St = max(0, St−1 + ℓt − k)  →  alarm when St ≥ h

It comes with a proof: under Lorden's criterion, CUSUM minimises worst-case detection delay for any tolerated false-alarm rate. Not a heuristic that felt right — the best possible answer to the speed-versus-false-alarms question.

Nobody else does this

A scan of the entire 562-hook UHI directory returns zero uses of CUSUM, change-point detection, SPRT or quickest detection. Everything out there triggers on moving averages and fixed thresholds — exactly what an attacker can time.

The firing moment is a data-dependent stopping time: a real trend crosses fast, noise never gets there. There is no "after N blocks" for an attacker to precompute.

Poincaré03
The design

A brain that decides, a curve that acts, a fee that breathes

The brain

Two-sided CUSUM detector

Runs on the pool's own log-returns, sampled once per block from pre-swap reserves, so flash tricks never reach it. A directional-efficiency gate confirms the move is a march rather than a thrash, and Huber-clipped increments stop fat-tailed crypto returns from fooling it.

The actuator

A directional spread LPs keep

Once a trend is confirmed, flow pushing with it pays a small, capped spread that goes straight to the LPs it would otherwise have been taken from. Flow that stabilises the pool trades at the full base price — punishing your friends is bad business.

The revenue

A fee generated from volatility

In calm markets LPs still earn: the base fee is computed each block from the pool's own realized volatility, min(γ·σ̂, cap). It tightens when things are quiet and widens when they are not — never a constant someone picked in a meeting.

Poincaré04
The actuator

Toxic flow pays a spread. Stabilising flow pays nothing.

We shipped the safe lever — and can show why

The obvious design, different curve depth per direction, is arbitrage-drainable. We built it, found a buy-shallow / sell-deep round trip that empties the pool, and rewrote the asymmetry as a non-negative spread on a symmetric base: safe by construction, and proven by fuzzing plus a 128k-call invariant per flavour.

Unlike a fee, it is targeted

A symmetric fee taxes everyone on every block. Poincaré charges only the side actually taking money out of LPs, and only while the detector is confident. The soft side trades at exactly the constant-product price — so there is never a prize sitting there for an attacker.

Cost to uninformed flow over the full year: 3,925 for Poincaré against 3,100 for the matched symmetric fee — targeting is what that gap buys.

Poincaré10
Why it can't be gamed

Faking a trend means genuinely moving the market, at your own expense

1 · No countdown to study

The firing time depends on the data, and the detector samples once per block from pre-swap reserves, so a flash loan that unwinds inside a block is simply invisible to it. There is no block number to precompute and no crossing to schedule.

2 · Nothing to win

The spread only ever makes trades worse, never better. The soft side trades at the plain base price, so max_soft_gain ≡ 0 — an attacker who fakes a trend finds zero edge waiting on the other side. Proven in the test suite, not argued.

3 · Arbitrageurs collect the toll

Pushing price off fair value opens a gap arbitrageurs harvest the moment the attacker stops. To move the statistic you must genuinely move the market, and that cost climbs every block while buying nothing.

All three layers are simulated end to end, against the real PoolManager

✓ A fake-trend round trip loses money after gas and modelled arbitrage.

✓ A single-block flash spike moves the detector by exactly zero.

✓ A σ-inflation attack on the adaptive mode burns value every block.

The security argument is not "we hope nobody tries". It is an inequality — max_soft_gain < min_trigger_cost — that the suite checks empirically on every run.

Poincaré11
Impact · what this unlocks

The detector is a platform, not a one-off.

Everything below runs on the same statistic — three of the four are already implemented and tested.

✓ built

Self-calibrating thresholds

Feed the CUSUM the standardised increment r/σ̂ and thresholds breathe with the market — scale-free across pairs, no governance. Cuts LVR 29.6% on the synthetic path against 14.3% for absolute mode, with no absolute calibration. Gated off until its manipulation bound is derived.

✓ built

Any pair, no code change

Point the calibration harness at another series and it re-derives σ, k and h from that pair's own returns. Every parameter is already a constructor argument.

the bigger prize

The curvature lever

The spread is the safe half of the idea. Moving depth per direction is a far stronger lever; it needs the manipulation-cost sizing that keeps it arb-safe — exactly the analysis the detector makes tractable.

✓ built

Routing that self-selects

The Lens quotes the true curve, so best-execution routers naturally send calm and counter-trend flow here and take toxic flow elsewhere — the LP-favourable split, as a consequence of pricing rather than a rule.

Why it matters beyond one pool

LVR is the largest structural tax on onchain liquidity. Every mitigation shipped so far needs an oracle, an auction, or trusted infrastructure. This one needs nothing but the pool's own price, so it composes with any v4 pool on any chain.

And it generalises

Any hook that must decide "is this regime real?" — dynamic fees, circuit breakers, sandwich detection — can consume the same statistic instead of a fixed threshold an attacker can time.
